# Data

## Source

[MetaboNet](https://metabo-net.org/data), the `metabonet_public` consolidated release
— the immediately-available, **no-DUA** portion. DUA-gated studies (T1DEXI, OpenAPS
Commons, Tidepool-JDRF) are not in it. Timestamps are de-identified and shifted.
Maintained by Replica Health Co.

Measured on the file this study used: 154,842,077 rows at 5-minute resolution, 1,291
subjects, 14 source studies, ~1.3 GB Parquet.

## What ships with this repository

| | where | size |
|---|---|---|
| Raw parquet | **not shipped** — `scripts/fetch_data.py` downloads and checksums it | 1.3 GB |
| Derived cohort (875 subjects, 182,597 windows) | `data/cohort/metabonet875/cohort.npz` | 58 MB |
| Fake cohort for tests | generated by `scripts/make_fake_data.py` | 7 MB, not committed |

The derived cohort is enough to reproduce the entire outlier stage without any
download. The raw parquet is only needed to rebuild a cohort with different rules.

**The pack is lossless.** `cohort.npz` holds the same float32 array as the 210 MB
`windows.npy` it was made from, deflated (`scripts/pack_cohort.py`, which verifies the
round-trip bit for bit). Dropping to float16 would have saved a further 6 MB and made
the shipped data differ from the data the checked-in results were computed on, in the
fourth decimal, forever. 58 MB is over GitHub's 50 MB advisory threshold and well under
its 100 MB hard limit: push warns, push succeeds.

## ⛔ The subject key is (source_file, id), not id

`id` is unique only **within** a source study — each study numbers its participants
from 1 and the consolidated release did not re-key them. 241 of 1,291 ids (18.7%)
appear under two or more studies, and those are different people: subject "102" spans
five studies and reports two CGM devices at the same minute with different values.
Full evidence in `docs/PITFALLS.md` §13.

Consequences for the shipped `metabonet875` cohort, which keys on `id` alone:

- **238 of its 875 subjects (27.2%) are composites** of 2–9 people, holding **41.0% of
  the windows**
- **9,055 windows (5.0%)** interleave two people sample by sample and carry
  `acf(2) > acf(1)`, which a smooth five-minute trace cannot produce
- the headline findings are *not* explained by this: consensus outliers are depleted of
  composites (2 of 24 against a 27.2% base rate, Fisher p = 0.9955), subject 1142 is
  single-study, and composites are not more identifiable (p = 1)

Re-keying is not only a correction — it **enlarges** the cohort, because one composite
becomes several subjects:

| | keyed by `id` | keyed by `(source_file, id)` |
|---|---|---|
| subjects in the file | 1,291 | **1,907** |
| CGM, ≥30 complete days | 875 | **1,329** |
| CGM+basal+bolus | 834 | **1,253** |
| CGM+basal+bolus+carbs | 805 | 945 |

Cohorts built on the corrected key are `data/cohort/metabonet_sid_c1` and
`metabonet_sid_c3` (`scripts/build_cohort_multi.py --subject-key study_id`). Every
oversized day turned out to be duplicate timestamps rather than finer sampling — 0% of
rows fall off the five-minute grid — so the builder projects each day onto the 288-slot
grid and keeps the first row per slot.

## Cohort construction

1. Keep (subject, day) pairs with all 288 five-minute cells present. This is stricter
   than the consensus 70%-capture bar and is what makes windows directly comparable.
2. Keep subjects with at least `min_days` complete days (default 30). Below that a
   subject's own distribution cannot be estimated, and four of the fourteen methods
   are distribution comparisons. The cost is small: the 222 subjects this drops hold
   0.8% of the windows.
3. Draw subjects **stratified on day count**. A subject's day count drives its
   nearest-neighbour distance independently of membership; if outliers happened to be
   the prolific subjects, the study would measure data volume.
4. Normalise: population z-score, clip to ±5, divide by 5.

## Channels: what is actually in the file

37 columns, of which most are per-subject constants (`age`, `height`, `weight`,
`gender`, …) or near-empty wearable streams. Measured by `scripts/channel_coverage.py`
over all 154,842,077 rows — `non-zero` is the fraction of the non-null cells that are
not exactly 0.0:

| channel | non-null | non-zero | subjects with ≥30 complete days, with CGM |
|---|---|---|---|
| CGM | 0.898 | 1.000 | 875 |
| insulin | 0.965 | 0.767 | 852 |
| basal | 0.936 | 0.751 | 839 |
| bolus | 0.944 | **0.066** | 835 |
| carbs | 0.710 | **0.016** | 808 |
| calories_burned | 0.251 | 0.034 | 366 |
| heartrate / steps | 0.007 | — | 8 / 6 |
| skin_temp / GSR / air_temp / workout_* | ≤0.001 | — | 0 |

**Non-null is not informative.** A pump writes `0.0` into every five-minute cell where
nothing was delivered, so `bolus` and `carbs` look denser than CGM and are in fact
event channels — 93% and 98% of their non-null cells are exactly zero. `basal` and
`insulin` are the genuine second continuous traces.

Wearables are not usable at all here: `heartrate` and `steps` reach the 30-complete-day
bar for 13 and 12 subjects respectively, out of 1,291.

### The 63% figure in the old cohort docstring is wrong

The single-channel design was justified by "the insulin channels … gate out 63% of the
subjects that have usable CGM". Under this repository's own complete-day rule the loss
is **4.7%**:

| combination | subjects | complete days | of CGM-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| CGM | 875 | 199,933 | 100% |
| CGM+basal | 839 | 178,558 | 95.9% |
| CGM+basal+bolus | 834 | 175,165 | 95.3% |
| CGM+basal+bolus+carbs | 805 | 143,942 | 92.0% |

CGM alone reproduces the shipped 875 exactly, so the two numbers come from the same
rule. The multi-channel subjects are not concentrated in one study — Loop 672→652,
DCLP3 92→92, DCLP5 26→26; only BrisT1D (14) and T1D-UOM (6) are lost entirely — so a
single-channel/multi-channel comparison is not confounded by source study.

Relaxing the per-day bar from 288/288 to 90% would give 1,207 multi-channel subjects
against 1,239 CGM-only, if a larger N is ever needed.

## Normalisation constants are per cohort — do not carry them over

Every clinical metric is defined in mg/dL and is recovered by inverting the transform
in `manifest.json`. Reusing another cohort's mean and sd leaves every TIR / MAGE / GRI
value **wrong but plausible, with nothing raised**. This has happened here: a page
served 402-subject 3-channel constants against a different cohort for weeks.

`cgmoutlier.data.cohort.to_mgdl` takes the manifest as an argument for this reason.
There is no module-level constant to reach for by mistake.
