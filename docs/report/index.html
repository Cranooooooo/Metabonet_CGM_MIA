MetaboNet · DiM-TS · membership inference

If you train a generative model on diabetes data,
does it leak who was in the training set?

We trained 123 generative models — two per subject, one that saw them and one that did not — and measured whether the released synthetic data leans toward that person. The answer: the effect is real and reproducible, but negligible in magnitude.

02

Two questions, two sets of measurements

This study answers two different questions. They use different data, different features and different metrics. The most common misreading is to answer one of them with the other's number.

Question 1 · Threat

Can a realistic attacker tell whether someone was in the training set?

What the attacker has
One released synthetic dataset, plus one person's real records. Not a second model trained without them — that does not exist in the real world.
Features
Ten distance statistics per window against the synthetic set: Euclidean family, k-nearest-neighbour family, cosine family.
Metric
Per-subject AUC, leave-one-subject-out cross-validation. 0.5 = cannot tell.
Answer
No. Best configuration +0.014; 79 of 80 subjects sit in 0.50–0.52.
Question 2 · Science

Are outliers memorised more readily than typical subjects?

What it needs
Two models per subject: one that saw them, one that did not. This is a research instrument, not an attack.
Statistic
gap = distance to the model that did not see them − distance to the model that did. Positive means information entered the model.
Metric
P(a random outlier's gap > a random control's gap). 0.5 = no difference.
Answer
Yes, but the magnitude is tiny. 0.664 across three replicates, while the distance itself moves by three parts in a thousand.

How the gap is computed

Drag the slider to see how the degree of memorisation changes the two distances. Each dot is a synthetic sample; the black marker is one real window belonging to this subject.

d_IN versus d_OUT

gap = d_OUT − d_IN. We take the distance to the single nearest synthetic sample — memorising a person shows up as one sample sitting unusually close to them, not as the whole distribution shifting toward them.

Three independent replicates, with and without subject 1142

1142 is the only subject whose personal AUC is clearly elevated. If the conclusion rested on him alone, removing him should collapse it to 0.5.

Does a different distance definition change the conclusion?

All six variants were computed, but which one counts was frozen in advance on the positive control. The split is on the horizontal axis (how a sample is chosen from the synthetic set), not the vertical one (how a subject's windows are pooled).

06

Four directions that led nowhere

Each was once a "maybe it's because…" hypothesis, and each was ruled out by measurement. Negative results are results — they cross off the candidate explanations one at a time.

Windows too short? — No

Using a verbatim-copying generator as a ruler: its detection ceiling is set by the sampling fraction alone, independent of how well anything fits.

Model too small? — No

The vertical axis is how accurately a discriminator separates real from synthetic; 0.5 is best (indistinguishable). One model per width, identical budget.

Too few channels? — Adding them made it worse

Same models, same attack; only which channels are kept changes. Left: arm AUC. Right: identifiability in the real data.

The fourth: not trained long enough? — Also no. The loss converges by 20,000 steps, and the next 70,000 improve it by 1.6%. In the seven-day round I initially matched epochs and trained only 3,700 steps, which was 20% underfit; those models were discarded and retrained to convergence, and the conclusion did not change.

07

Conclusion

The publishable claim

A bounded safety result: in CGM generation, membership leakage is measurable and systematic, but negligible in magnitude.

It cannot be stated as "there is no MIA problem" — that leaves 0.664 unexplained. And it cannot be stated as "outliers are identifiable" — the evidence does not support it; the strongest attack separates the two arms by +0.014.

Three things that hold the conclusion up

ClaimEvidenceWhy it is credible
The effect is real0.664 Three independent replicates (0.663 / 0.674 / 0.655); survives removing the extreme case
The magnitude is negligible0.27% vs 0.11% Relative change in distance; 79 of 80 personal AUCs fall in 0.50–0.52
The zero was measured0.82 / 0.50 The same instrument reads 0.82 on a memorising generator (ceiling 0.816) and 0.50 on a negative control

Two methodological findings that stand on their own

Finding A

Identifiability ≠ memorisability

You cannot predict which channel leaks from which channel identifies people. Basal rate carries 22× the identity information of CGM and contributes zero leakage.

Finding B

Multi-channel attacks must report per channel

Flattening channels into one Euclidean distance assumes they are homogeneous. Adding an uninformative dimension changes which sample is "nearest" and dilutes the real signal.
