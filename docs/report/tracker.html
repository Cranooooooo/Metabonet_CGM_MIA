Finding. Contrast peaks at 30,000 steps (arm AUC 0.840) and collapses to 0.515 by 100,000. Over the same range the number of patients at risk rises from 11 to 22 of 26 and Context-FID worsens 0.061 → 0.083. The outlier group holds at 9–9 of 13 throughout; it is the normal group that goes 2 → 13. Over-training does not expose outliers further — it exposes everyone. Between those two budgets, training longer is worse on every axis measured, so early stopping is a real defence our model has to beat.

3c — What kind of information leaks complete

The same attack recomputed after transforms that each destroy one kind of information. Arm AUC is unitless, so it can be read down the column — the raw gap differences cannot, because each transform changes the space the distance lives in.

Finding. Destroying all timing by sorting each window gives the strongest signal in every condition (mean arm AUC 0.838), while keeping shape and removing level and scale gives the weakest (0.550). The leak is carried by the distribution of glucose values, not by when events happen. Sorting is not adding information — it removes the day-to-day timing jitter that was swamping the membership signal, which also means a stronger attack than ours exists. For Step 4 this says the editing module should change the value distribution, not relocate events.
