Monitoring Data: Who Leaks, When, and Why\\
Training Longer Makes It Worse}

\begin{abstract}
Sharing continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data is constrained by privacy regulation,
and synthetic data from deep generative models is increasingly proposed as the remedy.
Whether that remedy holds has not been tested systematically for physiological time
series. We audit membership-inference risk on synthetic CGM data across 506 patients,
comparing 13 outlier patients against 13 controls matched exactly on record length,
under a $2\times2$ design that varies window length (one day, seven days) and channel
count (glucose alone, glucose with insulin). Three findings follow. First, the risk is
real but not selective: in the single-channel conditions almost every control patient is
identifiable as well as almost every outlier, and one patient is identified with
certainty. Second, risk and outlier--control contrast move in opposite directions as
training proceeds; contrast peaks early and then collapses, not because outliers become
safe but because controls catch up, so an audit performed at convergence understates how
distinguishable individuals are. Third, the leak is distributional rather than temporal:
an attack that destroys all timing information by sorting each window is stronger than
one that does not, which localises the exposed quantity to the distribution of glucose
values a patient produces rather than to when their glycaemic events occur. We then
propose a generator that acts on this localisation. The results bear directly on how
synthetic CGM datasets should be produced and audited before release.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{C}{ontinuous} glucose monitoring produces some of the densest longitudinal
physiological records in routine care: a reading every five minutes, sustained for
months, across large cohorts. Those records support work on glycaemic phenotyping,
closed-loop control and risk prediction, and almost none of it can be shared freely. The
constraint is not technical but regulatory and ethical, and it applies most tightly to
exactly the records that are most informative --- long, continuous, individual traces.

Synthetic data is the standard proposed remedy. A generative model is trained on the
real cohort, and its samples are released in place of the records. The appeal is that
the released data is not anyone's data. That argument is not a guarantee: generative
models retain information about their training sets, and a sample that resembles no
single patient in aggregate may still carry enough of one patient's structure to reveal
that they were used. This paper asks whether that happens for CGM, and if so, to whom
and through what.

Three gaps motivate the study.

\emph{Physiological time series are under-examined.} Membership inference against
generative models has been studied mostly on images and on tabular records, and neither
transfers cleanly. An image is a single sample; a patient contributes a set of windows
whose number varies between individuals, and that count is itself a confound --- a
patient with a longer record has more chances for one of their days to sit close to a
released sample, whether or not they were used in training. Tabular attacks assume
exchangeable rows, which consecutive days of one person are not.

\emph{The assumption that outliers are the population at risk has not been tested.} It
is intuitive and widely repeated: unusual individuals are memorised, typical ones are
absorbed into the population model. Privacy analyses accordingly focus on worst-case
individuals. Whether the typical patient is meaningfully safer is an empirical question
that, to our knowledge, has not been answered for physiological time series.

\emph{Privacy is evaluated at a single operating point.} An audit trains a generator,
samples from it, and reports one number. Training length is a free parameter of that
procedure, and no reason has been given why the value chosen for a quality-oriented
pipeline should also be the value at which privacy is worst, or at which a leak is
easiest to detect.

The contributions of this paper are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item A systematic membership-inference audit of synthetic CGM generation over a
$2\times2$ design in window length and channel count on a 506-patient cohort, with a
design constructed so that record length, statistic selection, per-run variation and
asymmetric screening cannot explain the result.
\item The finding that risk and outlier--control contrast are not monotone in training
length and move in opposite directions, with the mechanism identified: the control group
catches up rather than the outlier group receding.
\item A localisation of the leak in both time and information type, obtained by
decomposing the attack statistic itself rather than by training a separate probe, which
identifies the distribution of glucose values --- not the timing of glycaemic events ---
as the exposed quantity.
\item A generator whose editing stage acts on that localised quantity, evaluated against
both generation quality and membership risk.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Generative Models for Physiological Time Series}

Deep generative modelling of clinical time series has moved from recurrent adversarial
models to diffusion and flow-based ones, with recent work reporting fidelity sufficient
for downstream training. Evaluation in this literature is dominated by fidelity and
utility: discriminative and predictive scores, distributional distances, and
task-transfer accuracy. Privacy, where reported, is usually a nearest-neighbour distance
summary rather than an attack, which measures how close the released data is to the
training set on average but not whether a specific patient can be identified.

\subsection{Membership Inference Against Generative Models}

Membership inference asks whether a given record was in a model's training set. Against
generative models the attacker typically has only samples, and the standard family of
attacks scores a candidate by its distance to the nearest released sample. The
literature is largely image-based, with a smaller body on tabular health records. Two
methodological points from that work carry over and we adopt both: an attack statistic
must be fixed before results are seen, because plausible alternatives disagree; and a
positive control with a known ceiling is needed to establish that the pipeline can
detect membership at all.

\subsection{Privacy Evaluation of Synthetic Health Data}

Frameworks for evaluating synthetic health data increasingly pair fidelity metrics with
a privacy component, commonly a distance-to-closest-record statistic and sometimes an
attack. Two limitations recur. Reported numbers come from one training configuration, so
the trajectory is invisible. And where an attack is used, the control group is often
drawn as any record not in the flagged set, which is a much looser screen than the one
applied to the flagged group; if the two arms are screened at different strictness,
records of the flagged kind end up among the controls, both arms leak, and the contrast
the audit is trying to measure is suppressed. Our design screens both arms at matched
strictness for this reason.

\section{The Proposed Generator}

The generator has two parts: a flow-matching backbone that generates in an exactly
invertible transform space with a frequency-domain view, inherited from an existing
multi-view generator we refer to as MAVEN and reconfigured for CGM; and an editing stage
applied after the views are combined, which is ours and exists only for privacy. One premise
carries the design: the attack reads a \emph{tail} property of the released set --- the
distance from a real window to its \emph{nearest} synthetic neighbour --- whereas every
fidelity metric we report reads a \emph{bulk} property, so moving the tail while leaving the
bulk in place is not a point further along a trade-off curve.

\subsection{Backbone, inherited}

A window $x\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times C}$ is lifted by two maps $\Phi_A,\Phi_B$ into
two-dimensional token sequences: a \emph{gather} map from time onto grid cells, and a
short-time Fourier transform retaining real and imaginary parts with each frequency bin
affinely normalised. Both are exactly invertible and, critically, \emph{linear}; a
magnitude-only spectrogram would be neither. Linearity is load-bearing, because
$\Phi((1-t)x_0+tx_1)=(1-t)\Phi(x_0)+t\Phi(x_1)$ makes the linear flow-matching path exact in
transform space: with $x_1\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ drawn in the time domain,
\begin{equation}
z_t = (1-t)\,\Phi(x_0)+t\,\Phi(x_1), \qquad v^{\star}=\Phi(x_1)-\Phi(x_0),
\label{eq:fmpath}
\end{equation}
and each view is trained by mean-squared error between a predicted velocity
$v_\theta(z_t,t)$ and the target $v^{\star}$. That network is a Transformer over transform
tokens with a sinusoidal embedding of the flow time, under an exponential moving average;
sampling integrates the ODE by explicit Euler from $t=1$ to $t=0$, both views from the
\emph{same} time-domain noise draw so that their outputs are paired views of one sample, and
the decoded windows are then combined. All of this is inherited unchanged, and any
substituted view must keep invertibility and linearity.

\subsection{Reconfiguring the backbone for CGM}
\label{ssec:cgm}

MAVEN's published configuration targets benchmarks of 6 to 59 channels at $T=64$ to $256$,
whereas our cohort is $C\in\{1,2\}$ at $T\in\{288,2016\}$ sampled every five minutes with a
known exact daily period. Four changes follow, each licensed by a fact rather than a
preference. Three reuse a transform or a flag the backbone already implements; the fourth,
noted where it occurs, does not.
\emph{(i)} At seven days the gather view becomes a day fold: $2016=7\times288$, so the map
from cell $(r,c)$ to time index $288r+c$ is exactly invertible with no padding, and puts day
index on one axis and time of day on the other. It also halves the sequence the velocity
network attends over, $4024$ delay-embedding tokens at $T=2016$ against $2016$; measured
peak memory falls from $7.9$ to $4.0$\,GB at a micro-batch of 64, and attention cost falls
with the square. We state this as an efficiency argument and not a feasibility one: both
views train on a 40\,GB device, because the attention implementation does not materialise
the token-by-token matrix and memory is therefore linear in the token count rather than
quadratic. Upstream reaches the fold only as the alternative to the
spectral view and only for a period its own heuristic estimates, which is clamped to $67$ at
this length; ours is a day, known a priori, and is supplied directly. \emph{(ii)} The spectral view is retuned
from minutes to hours, its parameters fixed by pilot, because upstream's 16-sample window is
80 minutes at five-minute sampling and collapses the postprandial limb, the overnight drift
and the circadian component into the DC bin --- the band our transform sweep implicates,
since $z$-scoring each window, which destroys level and slow scale while preserving shape,
gives the \emph{weakest} attack in every condition (arm AUC $0.527/0.657/0.467$).
\emph{(iii)} MAVEN reports a Context-FID that plateaus with training and names its own
narrowing trunk as the suspected cause; Context-FID is our quality gate, so we commit to
running that ablation and adopting the winner, rather than asserting a fix for a plateau
nobody has reproduced on CGM data. \emph{(iv)} Two views are used rather than four, the
coarse--fine pair being redundant after the above.

The view combiner is treated separately, being the most direct route from training data into
a released sample. Upstream it is a learned low-rank residual editor, conditioned on the
per-cell mean, standard deviation and median of each sample's 16 nearest real \emph{training}
windows, whose shared channel basis exists to preserve cross-channel correlation --- a
rationale that is vacuous at $C=1$ and thin at $C=2$. We therefore combine, at both channel
counts, with the learned per-channel mixing weight the backbone already provides, and keep
the retrieval editor as a measured ablation. This pairing is ours rather than a setting:
upstream implements the mixing weight in the training script \emph{without} the masked
objective, and the masked objective in a script that emits one output per view and defers
combination to the retrieval editor. Each part is upstream code; running them together is
not an upstream configuration. That weight is not independent of the training
set, its logit being fitted against real batches; but its whole dependence on the training
data is $C$ global scalars, against a retrieval channel evaluated per sample, and only the
latter can carry an individual window's statistics into an individual released sample. Where
the editor is ablated, its index is built on a disjoint reference split of patients the
backbone never trains on.

A fifth change is of a different kind, and it is the one place where the published
configuration does not survive the move to CGM. MAVEN's defining addition over its own base
generator is a masked-imputation pass, whose stated purpose is to force the network to
represent $p(\mathrm{channel}_j\mid\mathrm{other\ channels})$ --- the weakness identified on
the high-channel benchmarks. The mask is drawn per channel across all tokens, so at $C=1$ it
is one coin flip per window: with probability equal to the drawn ratio the only channel is
masked, the network sees no observed values and is conditioned on its own detached prior,
which is self-distillation rather than imputation; otherwise nothing is masked and the loss,
normalised by a masked-cell count clamped below at one, is identically zero. Averaged over
the ratio's prior the task is an identically-zero loss on roughly 60\% of draws and
self-distillation on the rest, and it cannot learn inter-channel structure because a
one-channel dataset has no channel pairs.

We remove the degeneracy rather than the mechanism, by masking image cells rather than whole
channels: the mask becomes an independent Bernoulli draw per (token, channel) at the same
ratio, so at $C=1$ a random 20--60\% of a window's tokens are hidden and predicted from the
rest. The masked fraction is then that same 20--60\% on every draw instead of all-or-nothing,
and the objective takes the same form at both channel counts. At $C=2$ it \emph{subsumes} the
published one, since a cell masked in one channel may be predicted from the observed cell in
the other at the same token, and whole-channel masking is the degenerate corner of the same
distribution. It is also the missingness CGM exhibits: sensors drop individual readings, not
an entire modality for a whole window. Each view draws its own mask in its own image space,
so nothing is required to transfer between the gather and spectral views; and the change is
confined to training, the sampling path conditioning on an all-zero mask, so the released
pipeline is identical either way.

One consequence must be stated rather than assumed away. An objective that reconstructs
masked content from 40--80\% real observed context is closer to autoencoding than
unconditional generation is, and could raise memorisation rather than lower it. We therefore
ablate the pass at both channel counts and report it through the same attack, so that its
privacy cost, if any, is measured on the axis this paper is about rather than argued.

\subsection{What the edit has to move}

The attack was fixed in advance: for a subject $s$ with real windows $R_s$ and released set
$S$,
\begin{equation}
d(s,S)\;=\;\frac{1}{|R_s|}\sum_{i}\;\min_{k}\;
\frac{\lVert R_s[i]-S[k]\rVert_2}{\sqrt{TC}},
\label{eq:attack}
\end{equation}
and the score is the gap $d(s,S_{\text{base}})-d(s,S_{\text{member}})$. Its scale is small
--- the median gap is 2.5--4.9\% of the median nearest-neighbour distance at the
checkpoint of maximum contrast --- and its direction dictates the mechanism. The attack becomes
\emph{stronger}, not weaker, when all timing is destroyed: on per-channel sorted windows the
arm AUC rises from $0.562/0.698/0.627$ to $0.828/0.781/0.905$ across the three completed
conditions, all at $p<0.01$. We lead with the arm AUC because it is rank-based and
pre-registered, so it requires neither a normalisation nor an aggregation convention. A
relative-gap reading of the same comparison was computed twice, under different matching
subsamples and aggregations; the two agree on the nearest-neighbour distances to within 5\%
and on the direction of the effect but not on either arm's percentage, and an early reading
that the control arm's quantile-space gap was exactly zero did not survive recomputation ---
it is $+1.7\%$. We do not build on that percentage.

Sorting is the right coordinate for an exact reason: it returns the window's empirical
quantile function per channel, and $\lVert\operatorname{sort}x-\operatorname{sort}y\rVert_2 =
\sqrt{T}\,W_2(\mu_x,\mu_y)$ for the two windows' empirical value distributions, so the
leaking quantity is a Wasserstein-2 distance between per-window value distributions. This
reconciles the result with the hour-of-day decomposition rather than opposing it: a patient's
extreme values are realised in the morning, so it is the values that are memorised and the
hour is where they occur. Sorting can only contract distances, so a quantile-space margin
certifies a floor on the attack's own raw-space distance; but the sorted distance is
0.19--0.21 of the raw one here, so that certificate excludes the copy-paste regime and
nothing finer, and we claim no formal privacy guarantee.

\subsection{Why the edit is directed}
\label{ssec:directed}

To raise a nearest-neighbour distance from $d_0$ to $d_0+g$, an edit directed along the line
joining the real window to its nearest released sample needs $\lVert\Delta\rVert=g$, whereas
an isotropic perturbation needs
\begin{equation}
\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\text{iso}}=\sqrt{(d_0+g)^2-d_0^2}\;\approx\;\sqrt{2d_0g},
\label{eq:directed}
\end{equation}
because distance adds in quadrature. The ratio $\sqrt{2d_0/g}$ is arithmetic on the measured
$d_0$ and $g$ of the checkpoint sweep, and at the checkpoint of maximum contrast --- the
operating point at which we argue these quantities should be read --- it is $9.0$, $8.8$ and
$6.4$ in the three conditions: for the same privacy gain, a directed edit needs six to nine
times less perturbation than an isotropic one. This is the argument against defences that
spend their budget isotropically, such as additive output noise, DP-SGD gradient noise or
sampling-time dropout, which sit on the existing risk--quality curve by construction and cost
a retrain per operating point; it is why we add no training-time privacy mechanism.

Three qualifications belong with that figure. If distortion is second order in
$\lVert\Delta\rVert$, the same gain costs the square of the ratio, some 40 to 80 times less
fidelity --- but that is a prediction, not a result, and it is tested by sweeping the edit's
scale and reading the exponent of the fidelity degradation. We do not assume second-order
behaviour, since a generic displacement has a non-zero mean in embedding space and would cost
linearly; we engineer it, through the barycentric construction below. Second,
$\sqrt{2d_0/g}$ \emph{decreases} in $g$, falling to $3.4$ at the seven-day condition's
40k-step checkpoint, so the design is most advantaged in the regime a well-trained,
early-stopped generator is already in. Third, the argument is first order in one nearest
neighbour: after the edit another released sample may become the nearest, capping the gain
near the first-to-second nearest-neighbour spacing. That spacing is measurable on samples
already in hand, without training, and is the cheapest falsification of the proposal, so we
measure it first.

\subsection{Rank-preserving quantile editing}
% NAME PROVISIONAL: RQE (Rank-preserving Quantile Editing), per MODEL_DESIGN.md 2.6,
% superseding the earlier working name QuantileShift. Recorded there as a preference, not
% a finding. Settle before Results, where renaming stops being free.

For each channel $c$ of a sample $x$, let $\pi_c=\operatorname{argsort}x[:,c]$ under a
deterministic tie-break and $q_c=\operatorname{sort}x[:,c]$, an exact invertible
decomposition with $x[t,c]=q_c[\operatorname{rank}_c(t)]$. We edit $q_c$ by a monotone map
and reassemble with the original permutation, so every rank statistic survives exactly: the
time of the peak and of the nadir, the ordering of every pair of timepoints, the rank
autocorrelation at every lag, and the Spearman cross-channel correlation. Nothing moves in
time --- we edit the axis our measurements say carries an individually attributable signal,
and leave alone the axis they say does not.

The first of the edit's two terms moves a sample off any single training window's quantile
vector along the data manifold. Let $\mathcal{N}(x)$ be the $m$ nearest \emph{generated}
samples of $x$ in quantile space, an index over the released set and never over real data,
and with $w\sim\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha\mathbf{1}_m)$ set $\tilde q=\sum_j w_jq_j$. In one
dimension the Wasserstein-2 barycentre of a set of distributions is exactly the weighted
average of their quantile functions, so $\tilde q$ is a genuine barycentre of nearby
generated value distributions, monotone by construction. Mixing contracts second moments,
which a per-level affine recalibration corrects with constants fitted in closed form on the
released set; this is the step that removes the first-order distortion term.

The second term is the privacy displacement. Against an index over the training windows'
quantile vectors, for a sample whose nearest training quantile vector is $q^{R}$,
\begin{equation}
q'\;=\;\tilde q+\eta\,\rho(u)\odot\frac{\tilde q-q^{R}}{\lVert\tilde q-q^{R}\rVert},
\label{eq:repel}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ allocates margin over quantile level and $\eta$ is one global scalar in the
units of the gap $g$, making $\eta\approx g$ the natural starting point; monotonicity is
enforced by an isotonic projection whose activation rate is logged, its firing meaning $\eta$
is too large for that sample. We take $\rho$ uniform: our decomposition of the sorted-space
excess over quantile bands does find the top band enriched in all three conditions, which is
why $\rho$ exists at all, but it was computed under the superseded subsample above and its
stability is not established, so it licenses no particular shape. A fitted profile is an
extension gated on that check, with uniform $\rho$ as its control. Per window, the repulsion
is scaled by the window's local isolation in quantile space --- computable from the training
set alone, with no attack run and no membership labels --- so that an outlier's isolated
extreme days take most of the margin and the crowded bulk almost none. This is the capability
early stopping cannot have, training length being a global knob while our audit finds
exposure to be a stable property of particular individuals. It is equally the origin of the
design's most specific attack surface, since a repulsive channel carves a low-density region
around each protected window, so we report an adaptive local-density statistic beside the
frozen one; and it means the released pipeline is not retrieval-free, but queries a
training-set index per sample as the upstream editor does, to push away rather than to pull
toward.

Finally, $\eta$ is calibrated rather than maximised. The statistic is signed and the
pre-registered test one-sided, so over-editing makes $d_{\text{in}}>d_{\text{out}}$, drives
the one-sided AUC toward zero, and yields a perfect distinguisher for an attacker who knows a
defence is deployed. We therefore reserve a calibration split of patients the backbone never
trains on, form the nearest-neighbour distances from the released set to the training windows
and to the held-out windows respectively, and choose $\eta$ to minimise a two-sample
divergence between those distributions subject to a fidelity budget --- targeting exactly the
quantity the attack reads, at zero from both sides rather than as far as it will go. The
diagnostic that this succeeded requires the two-sided $|\mathrm{AUC}-0.5|$ beside the
one-sided pre-registered value, since a one-sided reading cannot distinguish calibration to
zero from overshoot into inversion. That reporting change amends a pre-registered protocol
and is declared here as pending; the frozen one-sided statistic is reported unchanged in any
case.

Because the stage is post-hoc and deterministic given $\eta$, a grid of $\eta$ applied to one
released set traces the whole risk--fidelity curve at no additional training cost, and a
configuration flag recovers the unedited release from the same training run and the same
noise draw. Two quantities it would be natural to quote alongside are not measured and are
not presented as results. From reading the upstream implementation, the existing editor's own
per-cell displacement appears to be of the same order as the membership signal $g$, which
would mean the editing stage already operates at the right scale; that is an estimate from
code, settled by one comparison once the backbone runs. And we expect the editor's
training-set retrieval to \emph{increase} the measured gap, which is why we move its corpus
to a reference split --- but no experiment here has tested that, and the three-way comparison
that would, between training retrieval, reference-split retrieval and the unfused branch, is
part of the experimental plan.
